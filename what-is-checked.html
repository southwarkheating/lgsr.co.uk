What a gas safety check covers

The full checklist, line by line, as it appears on your landlord gas safety record.

This is everything a Gas Safe engineer checks and records on your landlord gas safety record. It follows the Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998.

For each gas appliance

CheckWhat it means
OwnerWhether the landlord or the tenant owns the appliance
Make, model, type and locationWhich appliance it is and where it is
Flue typeRoom sealed (RS), open flued (OF) or flueless (FL)
Operating pressure or heat inputProof the appliance is burning the right amount of gas
Safety device correct operationFlame failure devices and other safety controls work
Ventilation adequateThe appliance gets the air it needs to burn safely
Flue flow testFumes go up and out of the flue
Spillage testFumes do not spill back into the room (open flued appliances)
Visual condition of flue and terminationThe flue is intact and ends in a safe place
Appliance servicedWhether a service was carried out on the visit
Safe to useThe engineer’s verdict for each appliance
Warning label affixedWhether an unsafe appliance has been labelled

For the installation

  • Gas pipework tightness test
  • Installation mechanically sound
  • Emergency control valve accessible, correctly positioned and labelled
  • Gas meter present, and working pressure checked
  • Protective equipotential bonding

Alarms

  • Number of smoke alarms, and whether they were tested
  • Carbon monoxide alarm tested

Defects and actions

Any defect is written down with the action taken. If an appliance is unsafe, the engineer follows the Gas Industry Unsafe Situations Procedure, which may mean turning it off and labelling it. See failed gas safety checks.

The record

The record lists the property, landlord, date of the check, the number of appliances tested, the next check due date and the engineer’s Gas Safe number. See a sample landlord gas safety record.
